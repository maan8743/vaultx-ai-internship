Remote work has fundamentally changed how companies think about office space and employee productivity.
 A recent survey found that 68% of employees report feeling more productive working from home, citing fewer distractions and no commute time as the biggest factors.
  However, many managers have raised concerns about weakened team cohesion and slower onboarding for new hires, 
  since casual hallway conversations and spontaneous collaboration are harder to replicate virtually. Some companies have responded with hybrid models,
   requiring employees to be in the office two or three days a week, though early results on whether this actually solves the collaboration problem remain mixed. Meanwhile, 
   commercial real estate prices in major cities have dropped as businesses downsize their office footprints, creating ripple effects across local economies that depend on office workers for foot traffic.
